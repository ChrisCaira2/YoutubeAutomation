# Channel Strategy: Game Day Brain

A second channel, kept apart from The Athletics Archive, for videos made with AI-generated pictures.

- **Niche:** the science and psychology of sports, for fans and players. What really happens in your brain and body when your team loses, when you play through pain, or when you can't stop watching. The owner chose this on 2026-11-09, modeled on youtube.com/@Dogunee97, which does the same for dog owners.
- **Format:** 8 to 12 minute videos in the second person, as numbered lists of 5 to 9 items. The hook reframes something the viewer has felt and teases one item by number. Each item covers a moment the viewer recognizes, the named idea behind it and what research found, a comparison that makes it click, and a one-line payoff.
- **Look:** hand-drawn pencil-and-watercolor pictures on white, a new one every 5 to 8 seconds. A mascot fan stands in for "you". Numbered title cards burst in behind a sweep of balls, and captions highlight each word as it's said. FLUX.1 schnell draws the pictures on this Mac for free.
- **Topics:** chosen by the owner, one video idea at a time, from the hub's New video button.
- **Honesty:** facts are sourced and checked like on the main channel. Every claim says what the research measured and in whom. Pictures are illustrations, not evidence.
- **YouTube rules:** no real, identifiable person, no logos, leagues or team names in any picture. Tick "made with AI" at upload.
- **Monetization:** YouTube judges mass-produced, repetitive content across a whole channel. A shared format is fine, but every video needs its own research and a script with real substance, never a template filled in.
